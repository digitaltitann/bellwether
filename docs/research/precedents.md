# Topic: precedents

## Summary

Research status as of 2026-09-01 (note: web-search budget was exhausted mid-task and several domains were blocked to WebFetch, so some items rest on secondary sources; these are marked medium/low confidence).

STOCK REWARDS. The surviving stock-reward programs are all either (a) subscription/interchange-funded debit rewards (Stash Stock-Back: 1% in stock on the first $1,000/month then 0.125%, on a $12/month plan; up to 3% at merchant-funded bonus partners), (b) affiliate/brand-funded shopping rewards paid as a broker deposit (Acorns Earn, 10,000+ offers, invested 60-120 days after purchase to absorb returns), (c) B2B white-label (Bits of Stock, now embedded in Q2 and Jack Henry Banno: free round-ups tier, 1% stock tier; claims stock rewards are 34x more effective per dollar than cash), or (d) one-time acquisition promos with lottery-style value (Robinhood $5-$200 with ~99% at $5 and a $1,500/yr cap; Webull 10 fractional shares of $3-$300 each; SoFi $5-$1,000; Moomoo fixed NVDA tiers; Public $20). The brand-loyalty-in-stock model (Bumped: 1-5% of purchases in that brand's stock) died in Dec 2022 after brands stopped funding it and it degraded to a low-payout cashback portal. Grifin survives only because it is user-funded ($1 per purchase auto-invested) with subscription fees. Tokenized stocks (xStocks/Kraken/Bybit, Ondo, Robinhood EU, Coinbase from Aug 2026) are securities, exclude US retail, and no program was found that pays tokenized shares for gameplay; xStocks' xPoints (Mar 2026) rewards traders/LPs only. Regulatory boundary: Massachusetts fined Robinhood $7.5M (Jan 2024) specifically over scratch-off free-stock lotteries, confetti, tap-to-climb games and referral stock rewards; SEC withdrew its broader DEP rule June 2025. Lesson: the asset must be delivered by a licensed broker-dealer (Apex is the common custodian), reward value must be fixed-ratio and non-lottery, and the funding source must be a real margin (interchange, subscription, affiliate commission), not the treasury.

CRYPTO-FOR-PLAY. Ad-funded sats games are alive but tiny per user: Fumb Games' Bitcoin Miner (20M+ downloads) pays via ZBD with a dynamic daily cap of ~330 sats (about $0.25/day at BTC $77,254 on 2026-09-01; inference), users report ~$3/day only at high levels. ZBD itself raised $40M (Jan 2026), is unprofitable, and is repositioning from rewards to licensed payments rails; THNDR pivoted to Lightning wagering (Clinch) citing app-store policy churn; Coinbase ended Learning Rewards May 27, 2025 after $150M; Sweatcoin's SWEAT is down 99.7% and free users earn under $1/year. Apple 3.1.5(v) bans crypto apps paying currency for tasks; Google Play requires in-game loyalty rewards to be fixed-ratio and never multiplied by game performance. Lesson: payout per hour must be capped below net eCPM revenue with a dynamic cap, and the reward should be real but small and delivered by a licensed third party.

GET-PAID-TO-PLAY. Mistplay ($550/yr cap, 2h/day/game cap, level-20 cap, 180-day expiry, selfie-video KYC at first redemption, $1-$3/hr), JustPlay (~80,000 coins = $1 floating with ad-auction prices, 15,000 coins per 30s ad, 2-min cooldown, $2 min), Swagbucks ($935M paid since 2010, 1 SB = $0.01) and Mode Mobile (40M users, $325M paid, points redeemable for gift cards, PayPal, and Mode Mobile shares) all fund rewards from advertiser/affiliate revenue and pay $0.10-$3/hr. Lesson: front-load rewards, cap annual value, tie the conversion rate to realized ad revenue, and verify identity only at first cash-out.

TELEGRAM TAP-TO-EARN. Every major token is down 95-99% from ATH (NOT -98.4%, HMSTR -97.6%, CATI -95.2%, DOGS -97.4%, MAJOR -98%, BLUM -99.1%). Hamster Kombat: 300M signups, 131M eligible (43%), 2.3M banned for automation (same-pixel taps, synchronized logins, 400 accounts to one exchange address), average payout under $50, and criteria changed at season end (PPH to keys/referrals), triggering a boycott. Catizen cut player allocation to 15% pre-launch. Lesson: an engagement-minted token with no external funding source is a pure sell-pressure machine; publish allocation rules before play, never change them retroactively, and use behavioral (not just device) sybil filters.

GAME-BROKER INTEGRATIONS. Invstr Fantasy Finance (2018) paid cash vouchers to trade on its own brokerage; Fidelity Youth pays a $50 funding bonus; Greenlight/Fortune City have no stock rewards. Single most important lesson for a stock-reward idle game: make the reward a fixed-ratio, broker-delivered fractional share whose dollar value is derived from realized ad/subscription revenue per player-hour (on the order of cents, not dollars), capped daily and annually, with pre-published rules and KYC at first withdrawal.
