# Topic: legal-eu

## Summary

Status as of 1 Sept 2026, from primary sources where possible. (1) MiCA: the game's own token is most naturally a "utility token" (Art 3(1)(9): access to a good/service supplied by its issuer). Art 4(3) takes an offer entirely outside Title II if the token is "offered for free", is a utility token for a good/service that "exists or is in operation", or is usable only in a "limited network"; but a token is NOT "free" if holders must hand over personal data or the offeror gets any fee or monetary/non-monetary benefit, so a sign-up-gated reward is unlikely to qualify as free and the "existing utility" limb is the safer hook. Art 4(2) (<150 persons per Member State, <EUR 1m/12 months) only waives the white paper, not Art 7 marketing rules or Art 14 conduct duties. Anyone providing exchange/custody/transfer of the token as a service needs CASP authorisation; the last grandfathering ended 1 July 2026 (ESMA 17 Apr 2026), and non-EU entities may not serve EU clients beyond narrow reverse solicitation. Art 2(4) excludes financial instruments, so the stock tokens sit under MiFID II, not MiCA. (2) MiFID II/Robinhood: Robinhood Europe UAB (Bank of Lithuania; brokerage licence 3 Apr 2025, CASP 29 May 2025, PI 12 Mar 2026) sells "Classic Stock Tokens" that its own KID (1 July 2026) calls an OTC derivative on a US share/ETP, risk class 7/7, no shareholder rights, no compensation-scheme cover, "suitable only for informed retail investors". Since 1 July 2026 a second product exists: on-chain "Stock Tokens" that are tokenised debt securities issued by Robinhood Assets (Jersey) Ltd, freely transferable ERC-20s, restricted for US/UK/CA/CH/UAE persons. Robinhood's own giveaway terms (24 June 2026) only hand tokens to onboarded, appropriateness-tested clients with a 180-day hold and "not available to the general public"; the 2025 OpenAI/SpaceX giveaway drew a Bank of Lithuania information request (7 July 2025) and OpenAI's disavowal, and ESMA (1 Sept 2025) warned tokenised stocks "typically do not confer shareholder rights". ESMA's 24 Feb 2026 statement says any cash-settled derivative giving exposure to an underlying "would likely fall in scope" of national CFD product-intervention measures regardless of name; those measures prohibit "monetary and non-monetary benefits" that incentivise retail trading and ESMA notes circumvention is prohibited. Inference: a third-party game handing out Robinhood derivatives as rewards is exposed to this ban and to MiFID product-governance/target-market and marketing rules; Robinhood, not the game, would be the regulated distributor, and the game would likely need a formal introducer/tied-agent or partnership structure. The Retail Investment Strategy (political agreement 18 Dec 2025, application ~2028-29) tightens inducements and finfluencer marketing but does not change this. (3) Gambling: Belgium still treats paid random rewards with monetary-value prizes as illegal gambling (fines to EUR 800k, prison); Austria (OGH 6 Ob 228/24h, 18 Dec 2025) and the Netherlands (Council of State 2022) hold that loot boxes embedded in skill games with no cash-out are not gambling; Germany requires a stake (Entgelt) and a prize of monetary value; UK "gaming" requires chance for "money or money's worth". A reward with real cash value (stock token, tradable crypto) satisfies the prize element everywhere, so the design levers are: no stake (free to play, no purchase that affects odds) and/or deterministic allocation (no chance). Free-entry promotional draws are lawful in most states but are regulated (NL code: free entry, EUR 100k cap, once/year; Italy: MIMIT notice 15 days ahead, bond, fiscal rep). (4) Consumer law: UCPD Annex I items 20, 28, 31 and the 2021 guidance on loot boxes; DSA Art 25 dark-pattern ban if the game is an online platform; CPC 2025 virtual-currency principles; Digital Fairness Act proposal expected Q4 2026. Age: brokerage onboarding is adult-only; 18+ gating is required in practice. (5) UK: crypto promotions regime since 8 Oct 2023 bans "free crypto" incentives; a non-transferable redeem-only utility token is outside "qualifying cryptoasset"; tokenised stocks are not offered to UK persons and CFD rules (COBS 22.5) ban inducements; full crypto regime applies 25 Oct 2027. (6) GDPR: EDPB blockchain guidelines v2.0 (7 July 2026) say keep personal data off-chain; AMLR (from 10 July 2027) fixes 5-year KYC retention and deletion, so brokerage KYC cannot be repurposed for the game. (7) Tax: no EU state exempts rewards; Germany allows the giver to pay a 30% flat tax (Sec 37b EStG); NL charges 37.8% gaming tax on chance-based prizes >EUR 449 (organiser withholds); France taxes third-party gifts at 60%; Ireland exempts EUR 3,000/donor/year; Lithuania exempts small prizes (EUR 200) and gifts (EUR 2,500). Robinhood's own terms push all tax onto recipients.

## Findings (45)

1. **[high]** MiCA Art 3(1)(9) defines a utility token as "a type of crypto-asset that is only intended to provide access to a good or a service supplied by its issuer"; Art 3(1)(16) lists the ten crypto-asset services (custody, trading platform, exchange for funds, exchange for other crypto-assets, execution, placing, reception/transmission, advice, portfolio management, transfer services); exchange services are defined as concluding purchase/sale contracts with clients "by using proprietary capital".
   - Source: https://www.mica.wtf/mica/title-i-subject-matter-scope-and-definitions-art.-1-3/article-3 (2023-06-09)
   - Relevance: The game token can be structured as a utility token, but any in-game or partner swap of the token for funds/other crypto run as a service to players is a CASP-licensable activity.

2. **[high]** MiCA Art 4(2) waives the white-paper obligations for offers to fewer than 150 natural/legal persons per Member State, offers with total consideration below EUR 1,000,000 over 12 months, or offers solely to qualified investors. Art 4(3) disapplies Title II entirely where the crypto-asset is offered for free, is a mining/validation reward, is "a utility token providing access to a good or service that exists or is in operation", or is usable only in a limited network of merchants. A token is NOT "offered for free" where purchasers must provide personal data in exchange or the offeror receives any fees, commissions or monetary/non-monetary benefits. Art 4(4): exemptions fall away if the offeror announces intent to seek admission to trading.
   - Source: https://www.mica.wtf/mica/title-ii-crypto-assets-other-than-arts-or-emts-art.-4-15/article-4 (2023-06-09)
   - Relevance: Inference: reward tokens gated behind account sign-up (personal data) or that generate engagement/ad revenue for the operator are unlikely to count as "free"; the safer exemption is the "existing utility" or "limited network" limb, and the token must never be listed on a trading platform if the exemption is to survive.

3. **[medium]** Inference from the Art 4(1) list of obligations (a)-(g): the Art 4(2) small-offer exemptions only remove the white-paper duties (drawing up, notifying, publishing), not the legal-person requirement, the Art 7 marketing-communication rules or the Art 14 offeror conduct duties; only the Art 4(3) exemptions (free/existing utility/limited network) disapply all of Title II.
   - Source: https://www.mica.wtf/mica/title-ii-crypto-assets-other-than-arts-or-emts-art.-4-15/article-4.md (2023-06-09)
   - Relevance: Relying only on the <150-persons or <EUR 1m thresholds still leaves the operator subject to MiCA marketing and conduct rules; the spec should target an Art 4(3) exemption.

4. **[high]** MiCA Art 7 requires marketing communications to be clearly identifiable as such, fair/clear/not misleading, consistent with the white paper, to state that a white paper has been published, and to carry a statement that the communication has not been reviewed by any competent authority and that the offeror is solely responsible; no marketing may be disseminated before the white paper is published where one is required.
   - Source: https://www.mica.wtf/mica/title-ii-crypto-assets-other-than-arts-or-emts-art.-4-15/article-7 (2023-06-09)
   - Relevance: All in-game and social promotion of the token must carry MiCA-style disclaimers unless the offer is fully outside Title II under Art 4(3).

5. **[high]** MiCA Art 2(4) excludes crypto-assets that qualify as financial instruments (and deposits, funds, securitisation positions, insurance and pension products) from MiCA; Art 2(3) excludes unique, non-fungible crypto-assets; Art 2(5) mandated ESMA guidelines on qualification of crypto-assets as financial instruments by 30 Dec 2024.
   - Source: https://www.mica.wtf/mica/title-i-subject-matter-scope-and-definitions-art.-1-3/article-2.md (2023-06-09)
   - Relevance: Robinhood stock tokens are MiFID financial instruments (derivatives/debt securities), so their distribution is governed by MiFID II and product-intervention rules, not MiCA.

6. **[high]** ESMA Statement ESMA75-113276571-1679 (17 April 2026): the MiCA transitional period expired across the EU on 1 July 2026; after that date any entity providing crypto-asset services to EU clients without a MiCA licence "will be in breach of EU law and must cease offering such services"; non-EU entities may not provide or solicit MiCA services to EU clients outside the narrow reverse-solicitation exception; consumers should verify providers in the ESMA Interim MiCA Register.
   - Source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-04/ESMA75-113276571-1679_Statement_on_the_end_of_transitional_periods_under_MiCA.pdf (2026-04-17)
   - Relevance: Any exchange, custody or transfer partner for the game token must already be a MiCA-authorised CASP; no grandfathering remains and a non-EU game studio cannot itself provide these services to EU players.

7. **[high]** MiCA Art 143(3): CASPs operating under national law before 30 Dec 2024 could continue until 1 July 2026 or until authorisation was granted/refused, whichever first; Art 143(2): for crypto-assets admitted to trading before 30 Dec 2024, Arts 7 and 9 apply to marketing communications published after 30 Dec 2024 and trading-platform operators must have compliant white papers by 31 Dec 2027.
   - Source: https://www.mica.wtf/mica/title-ix-transitional-and-final-provisions-art.-140-149/article-143.md (2023-06-09)
   - Relevance: Confirms there is no remaining transitional relief for a new token launched in 2026.

13. **[medium]** ESMA Executive Director Natasha Cazenave (Dubrovnik, reported 1 Sept 2025) warned that tokenised instruments "typically do not confer shareholder rights. This can create a specific risk of investor misunderstanding" and that "Most tokenization initiatives remain small and largely illiquid so far"; the World Federation of Exchanges wrote to the SEC, ESMA and IOSCO on 25 Aug 2025 urging that securities rules be applied and that platforms stop marketing tokens as equivalent to stocks.
   - Source: https://www.cryptopolitan.com/esma-says-tokenized-stocks-mislead-investors/ (2025-09-01)
   - Relevance: Game copy must never describe rewards as "shares" or "stock"; a July 2025 ESMA formal statement was not located - the September 2025 remarks are the closest ESMA position found.

14. **[high]** ESMA Public Statement ESMA35-243228190-8024 (24 Feb 2026): the CFD definition from ESMA Decision (EU) 2018/796 is "a derivative other than an option, future, swap or forward rate agreement, the purpose of which is to give the holder a long or short exposure to fluctuations in the price ... of an underlying ... that must be settled in cash or may be settled in cash"; the assessment "should be conducted for all derivatives offered irrespective of their commercial name"; a derivative giving exposure to an underlying "which is not exclusively settled physically, would likely fall in scope" of the permanent national CFD product-intervention measures, which include leverage limits, risk warnings, margin close-out, negative-balance protection and "the prohibition of monetary and non-monetary benefits"; "participating in circumvention activities to the product intervention measures is prohibited"; mass-marketing campaigns or initiatives aimed at inexperienced investors are inconsistent with the required narrow target market; appropriateness assessment and a PRIIPs KID are required.
   - Source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-02/ESMA35-243228190-8024_-_Public_statement_on_derivatives_in_scope_of_the_CFD_product_intervention_measures.pdf (2026-02-24)
   - Relevance: Inference: Robinhood's cash-settled Classic stock tokens plausibly meet this CFD definition (leverage is not an element), so giving them away as game rewards risks being a prohibited non-monetary benefit that incentivises retail derivative trading, and routing it via a third-party game could be seen as circumvention.

15. **[high]** The 2018 ESMA CFD measures (mirrored in permanent national measures) prohibit "any form of monetary and non-monetary benefits that aim at incentivising retail investors to trade CFDs or to trade larger volumes of CFDs, including bonuses related to opening new accounts"; benefits that are not trading incentives (e.g. lower fees for all clients) are allowed.
   - Source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-agrees-prohibit-binary-options-and-restrict-cfds-protect-retail-investors (2018-03-27)
   - Relevance: If the tokens are CFDs, a reward that only vests after a player opens a Robinhood account or trades is squarely the kind of benefit the measures ban.

17. **[high]** Council and Parliament reached political agreement on the Retail Investment Strategy on 18 Dec 2025: a partial inducement ban was dropped in favour of an inducement test (tangible client benefit, costs disclosed separately, strengthened best-interest duty) with Member States free to keep national bans; marketing must be fair, clear and not misleading with explicit attention to finfluencers; application roughly 30 months after Official Journal publication (18 months for PRIIPs changes); Council endorsement expected June 2026 with OJ publication expected in the second half of 2026 and application 2028-2029.
   - Source: https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/EU-Retail-Investment-Strategy-Political-Agreement-Key-Points-and-Implications-for-EU-and-non-EU-Firms (2025-12-19)
   - Relevance: Future-proofing: game-driven acquisition rewards paid by Robinhood could be tested as inducements/marketing under RIS from ~2028; influencer-style promotion inside a game will be scrutinised.

18. **[high]** ESMA technical advice ESMA35-42-1227 (29 April 2022) states that "Gamification techniques in trading apps and personal recommendations on social media may cause retail investors to engage in trading behaviour without understanding the risks involved" and recommends addressing online engagement practices "such as the use of gamification techniques by firms or third parties"; ESMA's discussion paper ESMA35-43-3682 (14 Dec 2023) covers gamification, nudging, dark patterns and finfluencers.
   - Source: https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-makes-recommendations-improve-investor-protection (2022-04-29)
   - Relevance: EU supervisors already frame gamified pathways into investing - including via third parties - as an investor-protection risk; an idle game feeding a brokerage is the archetype.

19. **[high]** IOSCO Final Report FR/07/2025 on Digital Engagement Practices (May 2025) finds that gamification techniques "such as badges, rewards, and celebratory messages - can provide immediate gratification for investors, thus potentially encouraging them to trade more frequently when it may not be appropriate"; FCA 2025 research cited by press found high-engagement app users traded 7x more and were 4.8 percentage points more likely to suffer large losses; ESMA's Common Supervisory Action ranks digital platforms as its second priority.
   - Source: https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD794.pdf (2025-05-19)
   - Relevance: Regulators treat reward loops tied to trading as harmful by design; the spec should decouple rewards from trading frequency.

20. **[high]** Digital Services Act (Reg. 2022/2065, fully applicable 17 Feb 2024): Art 25 prohibits providers of online platforms from designing interfaces that deceive or manipulate recipients or materially distort their decisions (dark patterns); Art 28 bans profiling-based advertising to known minors; "online platform" (Art 3(i)) is a hosting service that disseminates information to the public unless that is a minor/ancillary feature.
   - Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065 (2022-10-27)
   - Relevance: Inference: a single-player idle game is likely not an online platform, but public chat, leaderboards or a player marketplace could bring it in scope of the dark-pattern ban.

21. **[high]** UCPD (2005/29/EC, consolidated) Annex I item 20 bans describing a product as "free" if the consumer must pay anything beyond unavoidable response/delivery costs; item 28 bans direct exhortations to children to buy; item 31 bans creating the false impression that the consumer has won or will win a prize when there is none or claiming it requires paying money; Art 5(3) assesses practices targeting vulnerable groups (including by age) from that group's perspective; Art 3(4) gives sector-specific EU rules precedence.
   - Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:02005L0029-20220528 (2022-05-28)
   - Relevance: "Free stock" messaging, prize framing and any pay-to-claim step are directly regulated; copy and reward mechanics must be checked against Annex I.

22. **[high]** Commission UCPD Guidance 2021/C 526/01 (29 Dec 2021): "The presence of paid random content (e.g. loot boxes, card packs, prize wheels) should be clearly disclosed to the consumer, including an explanation of the probabilities"; algorithmically personalising loot-box timing or odds to target addiction-prone players "may constitute an aggressive practice"; Recital 9 UCPD lets Member States regulate gambling and "gambling regulators in BE, NL and SK have considered that certain types of loot boxes fulfil the conditions of gambling".
   - Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:52021XC1229(05) (2021-12-29)
   - Relevance: Any random reward mechanic needs odds disclosure and must not be personalised against vulnerable players.

23. **[medium]** The CPC Network published (March 2025) key principles for in-game virtual currencies under existing consumer law: show real-world prices, do not obscure costs, do not force purchase of unwanted bundles, give pre-contractual information, respect withdrawal rights, fair terms, and protect minors; the Digital Fairness Act consultation ran 17 July-24 Oct 2025 and a Commission proposal is expected Q4 2026 covering dark patterns, addictive design (reward loops, loot boxes), influencer marketing and personalisation, with possible adoption late 2027 and application 2028-2030; the Netherlands is pushing for a loot-box ban in the DFA.
   - Source: https://blog.promise.legal/lootbox-regulation-2026-game-studios/ (2026-07-17)
   - Relevance: In-game currency pricing and reward-loop design will face EU-wide rules within the product's lifetime; build price transparency and spend controls now.

24. **[high]** Austrian Supreme Court (OGH) 6 Ob 228/24h, decided 18 Dec 2025 (published Jan 2026): FIFA Ultimate Team packs are not gambling under the Austrian Gambling Act because loot boxes must be assessed within the overall game, success in FUT does not predominantly depend on chance, and FIFA Points have no monetary value outside the game and cannot be converted to real money; the ruling overturned two lower-court refund orders (~EUR 20,000) and aligns Austria with the Dutch Council of State (March 2022) against Belgium (2018).
   - Source: https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/supreme-court-shifts-the-playing-field-lootboxes-fall-outside-the-austrian-gambling-act (2026-01-29)
   - Relevance: The court's reasoning turned on no cash-out; a game whose random rewards have real monetary value (stock tokens) cannot rely on this precedent.

25. **[medium]** Belgium: the Gaming Commission's April 2018 decision treats paid loot boxes as games of chance under the Gaming Act of 7 May 1999 (game, stake, chance, gain/loss); operating unlicensed games of chance carries criminal fines up to EUR 800,000 and up to five years' imprisonment; major publishers removed paid loot boxes; LS v Apple (Antwerp Enterprise Court, Jan 2025) extended liability to distribution platforms.
   - Source: https://blog.promise.legal/loot-box-laws-game-developers/ (2026-05-09)
   - Relevance: Belgium remains the strictest EU state: any paid mechanic with chance and a valuable prize is criminal there; rewards must be stake-free or deterministic, or Belgium geo-fenced.

26. **[medium]** Netherlands: the Council of State (March 2022) reversed the Ksa's EUR 10m fine against EA, holding loot boxes integrated in gameplay are not a standalone gambling product; the Dutch government has since shifted to seeking an EU-level loot-box ban via the Digital Fairness Act.
   - Source: https://blog.promise.legal/lootbox-regulation-2026-game-studios/ (2026-07-17)
   - Relevance: Dutch gambling law is currently not the binding constraint for loot boxes, but promotional prize rules and gaming tax still apply to chance-based rewards.

27. **[medium]** Germany: under the Interstate Treaty on Gambling (GlüStV 2021, §3) gambling requires consideration (a stake of not insignificant value) for a chance to win where the outcome depends wholly or predominantly on chance, and a prize with real monetary value; loot boxes have generally fallen outside because items lack external monetary value; free-to-enter promotions with no stake are not gambling; loot-box presence affects USK age ratings since the 2021 Youth Protection Act reforms. (Primary text not retrieved in-session.)
   - Source: https://programminginsider.com/loot-boxes-regulation-and-where-the-line-sits-in-2026/ (2026-01-01)
   - Relevance: In Germany the stake element is the lever: a free-to-play idle game with no paid entry to chance-based rewards stays outside gambling law even if rewards have value.

28. **[medium]** Spain published a draft law (July 2022) to regulate "random reward mechanisms": prohibition for under-18s with ID verification, mandatory spend/time limits and self-exclusion, gambling-style advertising restrictions, odds disclosure, DGOJ supervision and fines up to EUR 3m; the draft was withdrawn by the ministry in early 2024 and no replacement law was found as of Sept 2026.
   - Source: https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/propiedad-intelectual/art/espana-primera-regulacion-de-las-loot-boxes-o-cajas-botin-en-espana (2022-09-06)
   - Relevance: Spain currently relies on its Gambling Act (chance + stake + prize) and consumer law; a revived draft would impose 18+ and spend controls on random rewards.

29. **[high]** Netherlands promotional games code (Gedragscode promotionele kansspelen, enforced by the Kansspelautoriteit): a chance-based promotional game needs no licence if participation is free (only communication costs may be charged), it promotes a product/service/organisation, the prize pool is at most EUR 100,000 per game, and it is run at most once per year per product/service/organisation; small games under EUR 4,500 face lighter rules.
   - Source: https://kansspelautoriteit.nl/kansspel-om-iets-te-promoten (2026-09-01)
   - Relevance: A chance-based token or stock-token giveaway to Dutch players must be free to enter, capped at EUR 100k and annual - incompatible with continuous random drops; deterministic rewards avoid the code.

30. **[high]** Italy: prize contests (concorsi a premio, DPR 430/2001) require a notice to MIMIT at least 15 days before launch via the PREMA system (form CO/1), a bond equal to 100% of the prize value valid one year after the contest, a notary or Chamber of Commerce official for draws, a maximum duration of one year, and an Italian fiscal representative for foreign promoters.
   - Source: https://www.mimit.gov.it/it/mercato-e-consumatori/concorrenza-e-commercio/manifestazioni-a-premio/concorsi-a-premio (2026-09-01)
   - Relevance: Any chance- or skill-based prize mechanic reaching Italian players triggers pre-notification and bonding; deterministic in-game rewards should be designed to fall outside the prize-contest definition or Italy geo-fenced.

31. **[high]** UK Gambling Act 2005 s.6: "gaming" means playing a game of chance for a prize, a game of chance includes games mixing chance and skill, and "prize" means "money or money's worth"; s.14 defines a lottery as requiring persons to pay to participate with prizes allocated wholly by chance (skill filters that do not deter a significant proportion are disregarded); Sch.2 para 2 treats "transferring money's worth" and paying for goods at a price reflecting participation as payment.
   - Source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/19/section/6 (2005-04-07)
   - Relevance: Inference: a reward with cash value (stock token, transferable crypto) is money's worth, so any chance-based allocation that players pay to access (including via in-app purchases that improve odds) is gaming or a lottery in the UK; free entry or deterministic allocation avoids it.

32. **[high]** UK loot-box policy: the Gambling Commission holds that in-game items "confined for use within the game and cannot be cashed out" are unlikely to be licensable gambling; the government's July 2022 response declined to extend the Gambling Act, backed Ukie's July 2023 industry principles (parental controls for under-18s, spend controls, odds disclosure) and in January 2026 confirmed it is "keeping possible future legislative options under review"; the Commons Library briefing is dated 13 Aug 2024.
   - Source: https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-8498/CBP-8498.pdf (2024-08-13)
   - Relevance: The UK safe harbour depends on no cash-out - which real-asset rewards break.
